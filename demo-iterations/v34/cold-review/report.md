## Authenticity, Meta attribution, accessibility, and craft

The dialogue and posts are explicitly prepared examples for fictional participants. The Day 4/9/16 sequence is explanatory, not observed time passing. The “family” to “parents” correction and changed need show intended states, not verified model transformation. The group question is previewed but not sent. The film says no provider is verified/bookable.

Muse Spark is named as a planned organizer of correctable reflection; optional Muse voice transcription is described as a way reviewed speech could become text. That role suits a sensitive conversation where users should amend interpretations. But no live call, transcription, generated answer, model reasoning, or service integration is shown. Content is repeatedly labeled prepared/local.

Captions are high-contrast and generally readable, but their large bands cover app content; body text shrinks in full-page views. Motion is restrained, with little visual change between repeated scenes. AAC playback was unavailable, so narration quality and audio accessibility cannot be judged. No fast motion or flashing is apparent in sampled frames.

4. **1:10–1:40 — Make consent and exit distinct.** Show exact scope, consent enabling continuation, then cancel and return to the private draft, clearly labeled as illustrative. Expected: the sharing and no-share paths are undeniable. Verify the no-send outcome on screen.
5. **2:40–2:58 — Strengthen the payoff.** The privacy statement resolves in dense UI. Hold on Arjun’s choice with a short summary of what stays private and what he may share. Expected: emotional closure without claiming real outcome. Verify the final image is readable and held long enough.

## Judge reactions and release recommendation

**Skeptical objection:** “This is a careful consent prototype, but the AI and community value are not connected. Sample dialogue and multi-day activity are authored, and no actual person, model, or peer response appears. The long screen capture makes a concept feel more proven than it is.”

**Best-case reaction:** “The product understands that a sensitive family decision should stay with the person. It offers language the user can correct, excludes private context from a group draft, previews exact scope, and treats silence or unavailable help as real possibilities. Even as a prepared prototype, that is a humane product story.”

**Recommendation: revise before release as a competitive hackathon film.** Keep developing the concept and preserve its clear fictional/prepared labels. Do not present this cut as evidence of live AI or community function. The primary blockers are repetitive text capture, an unshown editing action, and the lack of a human-scale payoff.
