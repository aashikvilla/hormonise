# Harmonise v37 — creative brief

## Creative target

Make v37 a shorter, character-led film about one usable promise: **Harmonise helps an adult find their own words for a difficult family conversation, privately, and choose whether to share them.** The film should feel like a brief moment of a person making a decision, not a guided tour of feature pages.

Keep the evidence honest: this is a fictional scenario through a local prototype; the content is prepared, no model is connected, community posts and moderation are not live, no provider is verified, and nothing is sent. Put each disclosure where the relevant thing appears. Never imply an observed user outcome or a working Meta call.

**Target runtime: 88–95 seconds**, down from v36’s 126 seconds. Keep the same family-pressure situation so the improvement can be judged against v36, but use one continuous, user-operated correction as the film’s visual spine. The correction must be recorded from the actual frontend if it is reliable. If it cannot be captured faithfully, use a plainly labeled “prepared prototype flow” capture; do not fake typing, cursor movement, model activity, or state changes in post.

## Source assets and edit requirements

- Capture the correction interaction directly from the current local frontend with the browser chrome and unrelated desktop UI hidden. Record the complete click → edit → save → changed reflection sequence as a single take, including a short hold on the final state. Retain the original capture as the source of truth; don’t reconstruct a more polished interaction in motion graphics.
- Use the existing fictional character and reflection content only if the text remains consistent with the film’s opening. Create no testimonials, real-looking profile claims, success metrics, real provider credentials, or fabricated model traces.
- Prepare only two contrast cards and one moderator boundary line for the social beat. Keep the text and frame labels visibly marked as prepared fiction. Avoid stock footage that could make the fictional character or peer stories look like actual participants.
- Capture the exact audience/share preview and final private/unsent state at 1080p or higher, with cursor states included where they prove the choice. Use crops and simple cuts in the edit; do not add UI state transitions that did not occur in the frontend.
- Use a single visual style for disclosure labels. Keep labels readable but subordinate: **Fictional scenario**, **Prepared example**, **No model connected**, **Nothing sent**. A label must appear with its relevant content, not in a disclaimer slate viewers may miss.
- Deliver a narration mix and caption file/ burned-in-caption master with the same wording and timing. The v13/v36 blind reviewers could assess captions but not audio, so the meaning must work with sound off. Keep captions to at most two lines, away from the critical UI, and leave key interaction labels unobscured. Record narration after picture lock; use a restrained room-tone bed and light interface sound only where it reinforces an actual user action.

## What changes from v36, and why

V36 already had a good opening premise, correction choices, audience preview, privacy exclusions, and a no-send ending. Repeating those screens more cleanly is unlikely to lift the film much: the blind review still saw 126 seconds of mostly static pages, did not see a person operate the correction, found the AI role proposed rather than evidenced, and described the human payoff as fictional and restrained.

V37 should retain the same truthful claims but change the film’s evidence and shape:

1. **Show the promise by 14 seconds** in one sentence, tied directly to the human problem. The reviewers asked for a product benefit that a cold viewer can repeat, not another overview diagram.
2. **Spend 20 seconds on a verifiable, continuous correction**. A real cursor, edit, save, and changed reflection make agency concrete and give the film motion. The “prepared prototype” and “not generated by a model” labels make the limits equally clear; this improves evidence of frontend behavior without claiming model execution.
3. **Give peer value one contrast and one boundary**. A single perspective that differs from the character’s path, paired with the moderator’s listening boundary, is more useful than a long prepared thread and stays credible as fiction.
4. **Collapse sharing and support into one pass**. The exact preview, unchecked consent, disabled send, and brief provider limitation answer the important questions without spending nearly a third of the runtime on forms and availability screens.
5. **Make “Maybe later” the final human decision**. Hold on the person’s private words and unsent draft, then end on a short line that is easy to remember. This gives the ending a concrete choice rather than another interface summary.

These changes target the repeated weaknesses in both blind reviews: value not sharpened enough in the first 15 seconds, no continuous human-operated product interaction, a long static UI tour, and a quiet payoff. They may improve clarity, pace, and perceived frontend evidence; they do not solve the absence of connected model execution, live peers, verified providers, or observed outcomes, so no score increase or finalist outcome is guaranteed.

## Release gates

Release this cut only if all gates pass on the actual exported film:

- **15-second cold recall:** At least 4 of 5 first-time viewers can state both the audience/problem and the promise (“private reflection they can correct, with optional sharing”) after seeing only 0:00–0:15.
- **Interaction proof:** At normal playback speed, all 5 viewers can identify what the user changed and see that the reflection updated in the uninterrupted shot; none describe it as a live model response.
- **Truth check:** All 5 viewers understand the scenario and peer content are fictional/prepared; none believe a live model, peer exchange, moderator service, provider, or message send was shown.
- **Share/exit recall:** At least 4 of 5 can name the audience preview and recall that consent stayed unchecked and nothing was sent; at least 4 of 5 recall that waiting/keeping the reflection private is an available choice.
- **Runtime and readability:** Film is 88–95 seconds; no dense UI screen is held longer than needed to read its one decision; all captions and key UI text remain readable at ordinary full-screen playback without pausing.
- **Narration-caption match:** Every spoken claim is represented in captions, and captions contain no claim stronger than the picture or prototype supports. Review once muted and once with sound on.

If a gate fails, revise the cut or capture before export. Keep the claim boundary explicit: the strongest substantiated claim is that the local frontend lets a fictional user correct a prepared reflection and inspect a private/share choice.
