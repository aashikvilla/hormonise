# Independent first-pass video review: Harmonise

## Executive decision

**Decision: Maybe.** The film presents a thoughtful prototype for people under family pressure. Three strengths support keeping it in consideration: it names a specific dilemma; lets the user reject and rewrite an interpretation; and previews sharing scope before anything is posted. The reservation is clear: the film labels its content prepared and fictional, and says no model is connected. It demonstrates interaction design and a proposed AI role, not model execution or validated outcomes.

## First 15 seconds

At 0:00 the screen reads, “I can’t tell my family what I want without feeling ungrateful,” and labels the example fictional and prepared. By about 0:05, a caption states the promise: “For people navigating family pressure: clarify privately, correct the reflection, then choose whether to share.” This makes the audience and core distinction understandable: private reflection and user choice precede optional sharing. The opening does not specify user age or service setting, and the product name alone does not explain the wider scope. Confidence: high for problem and promise; medium for audience boundaries.

## Scene audit

- **0:00–0:10:** A fictional scenario introduces the three-step premise. The prepared/no-model label is visible from the outset, helping calibrate claims.
- **0:10–0:25:** Arjun types his concern in a private chat. A prepared response invites him to proceed slowly. On-screen text says chats do not appear in communities and the example stays in the browser session. The response could be mistaken for generated output if the small disclosure is missed.
- **0:30–0:55:** A “thought to check” offers “Yes, that’s closer,” “Not quite,” and “Edit in my words.” Arjun rejects it and supplies wording; the reflection changes. This is the clearest agency moment. The screen calls it prepared and says no model is connected; Muse Spark is described as planned.
- **0:58–1:20:** The experience sets aside broad legal topics, surfaces “Family Expectations & Personal Choices,” and opens a community preview. It says “No live activity” and “This is not a live community.” Ravi’s different path adds context, but prepared stories cannot establish peer benefit.
- **1:30–1:50:** “Ask Ravi in the group” shows an alias, exact audience, and question; private conversation is excluded. Arjun edits the question and cancels, keeping the draft. A later thread has no reply, and the caption says no response is promised. This is a credible recoverable outcome, though the interface text moves quickly.
- **2:00–2:24:** Arjun can keep a takeaway private, offer one helpful thing, or wait. He edits the contribution and leaves consent unchecked. The final state shows a private takeaway and optional unposted draft, then states nothing is sent. This is a strong consent demonstration, not a real post.

The main missed inference is how the experience handles varied, unprepared language. One prepared scenario cannot show recovery from an ambiguous or harmful suggestion. Captions communicate the intended limits, but the interface is text-dense at 1080p.

## Authenticity, Meta attribution, and craft

The film repeatedly distinguishes fiction from activity: “prepared,” “no model connected,” “no live activity,” and “nothing is sent.” The transformation from tentative interpretation to revised wording is shown, but the content is prepared; the film does not establish that a model produced it. Muse Spark is proposed to organize a correctable reflection, and optional voice transcription is also described as planned. This role is plausible, but the film does not show a live call or prove that an LLM is needed. Community stories and Ravi’s response path are illustrative, not social activity. Spoken narration could not be assessed. Visible captions are consistent and readable; smaller interface copy is harder to read in the full-frame video. Motion is restrained. The emotional high point is Arjun’s correction; the no-reply state keeps the outcome unresolved.

## Five highest-impact fix tickets

1. **0:10–0:35 — Separate prepared output from generation.** Keep the no-model label beside the response; in a future truthful live run, use a distinct state label. Expected result: viewers identify what is simulated. Verify with both clearly labeled states.
2. **0:30–0:55 — Show correction with unprepared input.** Record a real or clearly prepared input, rejection, and revised result with provenance labeled. Expected result: viewers can judge correction behavior. Verify by holding the full sequence onscreen.
3. **0:58–1:20 — Clarify community value and limits.** Hold “not live” and “no live activity” beside the example cards. Expected result: peer context is not mistaken for outcomes. Verify with a readable close view.
4. **1:30–1:50 — Slow the sharing review.** Show alias, audience, exact question, private-context exclusion, and cancel at readable scale. Expected result: privacy controls are clear without relying on captions. Verify by recording each edit and cancellation.
5. **2:00–2:24 — Strengthen the ending and accessibility.** Hold the private-versus-unposted state; retain captions and make narration accessible or unnecessary. Expected result: viewers absorb the consent outcome. Verify at normal playback size.

## Skeptical judge objection

“This is a carefully scripted UX concept, not evidence of an AI product working. Interpretation, correction, community discovery, and sharing controls are presented as prepared or fictional. Without a truthful model run or real peer interaction, I cannot award execution or claim impact.”

## Best-case reaction

“This prototype understands that people under pressure need time, control, and a way to revise what they share. It makes correction, audience review, cancellation, and silence visible instead of promising a tidy resolution. Its disclosures set a useful standard for a future implementation.”

## Release recommendation and blockers

Recommend continued prototype development, with a **Maybe for shortlist consideration**. The film supports the consent and interaction design as a plausible frontend prototype. Blockers to a stronger product claim are no demonstrated live model execution, untested response quality, and no real community activity or outcomes. These are evidence gaps in this film, not findings about unseen systems. No backend, production integration, or live API is credited or required for this phase.
