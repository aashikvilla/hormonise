Its strongest evidence is an on-screen rejection and correction of a reflection, followed later by a sharing preview with consent unchecked and a cancel path (30–43; 67–83). Those make a credible prototype case for user agency. The limits are equally explicit: “Fictional prepared demo · no model connected · no live community.” Muse Spark and community support remain proposed. An AAC track exists, but I could not hear it, so audio judgments are reduced.

## Authenticity, Meta attribution, and accessibility

Dialogue and stories are fictional/prepared. The correction appears progressive and coherent, but cannot establish live model behavior. Muse Spark is proposed to organize themes for Arjun to review (24–30); the need is a reviewable starting point, but the film substantiates only a prepared correction flow (30–43), not generated output. Community members and peer replies are illustrative, not live. Its strongest substantiated claim is a designed prototype flow with user review and consent controls.

Captions explain actions and are generally readable. Small page text reduces access; the consent details remain together, but a close view would help. Motion is mostly typing and page changes. The emotional arc is quiet: uncertainty, correction, then a private takeaway. The AAC stream is present but could not be heard, so narration, music, mixing, and audio-caption alignment are unjudged.

## Five highest-impact fix tickets

1. **0–15:** Audience and promise compete with a full-page view. Replace with a high-contrast card: “For adults facing family pressure: reflect privately, correct the framing, choose whether to share,” held three seconds. Expected: immediate audience and promise comprehension. Verify an unobstructed three-second hold.
2. **30–43:** Correction is visible but small. Use one uninterrupted close shot showing “Not quite,” caret, full typing, and changed reflection. Expected: unmistakable user authorship. Verify no transition between choice, typing, and update.
3. **67–78:** Consent details are together but small. Hold a closer view with audience, alias, question, exclusion note, unchecked box, and cancel visible for four seconds. Expected: scope and decline are easy to inspect. Verify one shot retains all six details and the unchecked state.
4. **24–30; 44–57:** Prepared/planned disclosures compete with interface detail. Make “Planned / not connected” and “Illustrative example / no live activity” prominent. Expected: proposed and shown behavior stay distinct. Verify legibility in the 1080p export.
5. **0–105:** This review could not access AAC. Provide full captions and a clean voice mix. Expected: understandable with sound off and usable with sound on. Verify captions against the complete narration and play the final AAC.

## Skeptical judge objection

This is a thoughtful consent-centered prototype, but the named model is not connected and the community is not live. Prepared examples and a polished interface do not show reliable AI interpretation, actual participation, or reciprocal benefit. The entry should be judged as a frontend prototype rather than an operating service.

## Best-case reaction

The interaction thesis is strong: let a person reject a framing before it becomes advice, show exactly what would be shared, and make privacy-preserving cancellation easy. The changed reflection and open-ended ending preserve agency rather than manufacture resolution. If the proposed roles are later validated, this could address a real and specific need.

## Release recommendation

**Maybe.** Suitable for discussion as a frontend prototype. Explicit blockers to a stronger film-based recommendation are no truthful model execution, no live community activity, and inaccessible audio evidence.
