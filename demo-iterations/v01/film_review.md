# Cold review — Harmonise v01

**Decision: Reject this submission in its current demonstrated form.** This is a thoughtful product direction with a coherent and unusually careful consent vocabulary, but the film demonstrates a prepared local simulation rather than a connected AI experience. My decision rests on three points: (1) the need is human and recognizable, but the first seconds do not explain it quickly; (2) no Meta model use is demonstrated, and the ending explicitly labels those integrations as planned; (3) the extensive workflow is presented with enough disclosure to be credible, but it supplies no implementation evidence for a working submission. The concept is worth developing, but the film does not establish a functioning Meta-powered entry.

**Evidence mode and confidence.** I reviewed the MP4’s picture and burned-in captions through sampled frames across its 179-second duration. I could not listen to the audio in this environment, so I do not claim to assess narration, music, sound quality, or spoken delivery. I used captions and visible interface text only. Confidence is medium-low: there is broad visual coverage, but inaccessible sound and the film’s prepared-example format limit judgment of pacing and function.

## First 15 seconds

The opening briefly shows “HARMONISE · CONCEPT DEMO” and “Opening the guided demo...” before reaching “You can start anywhere” and a private conversation interface. The viewer can infer a reflective chat, but not yet who needs it or what decision it helps with. Captions introduce family pressure (“At home, everyone wanted an answer”) and jump across Day 1 and Day 4. The privacy claim and later “not just similar words, similar situations” comparison are useful differentiators, but arrive after the opening hook window.

## Scene audit

- **00:00–00:15:** A blank/near-blank loading card delays the experience, then the interface offers a Day 1/Day 4/Day 9/Day 16 prepared history. The timeline implies a gradual process and the fiction/sample label helps prevent false claims. Missed inference: private reflection, peer support, or both?
- **00:30–00:50:** “Have I got this right?” shows the user’s words, a tentative interpretation, and “Not confirmed yet,” with “Yes, that’s closer / Not quite / Edit in my words.” A review-before-sending panel lets a person revise a typed transcript and says voice transcription is not connected. This is the strongest agency mechanism: interpretation is correctable and no audio is shared here. Captions overlap controls and message text.
- **00:50–01:20:** The community discovery page contrasts a broad result about separation logistics with a confirmed context about family expectations, explains why a match may fit, and offers “Not for me: change these results.” This is a legible reasoning surface and a distinctive premise. The material appears prepared; no model output or retrieval operation is shown.
- **01:20–01:50:** The community page repeatedly labels participants and posts illustrative, says there is no live activity, and explains the group is only a browser example. “One person’s experience” presents Ravi’s account as one perspective, not advice. The question flow previews the exact group, alias, and text, says a private conversation is excluded, and warns that an alias does not guarantee anonymity. This is the film’s best trust design. The prepared answer says it was not generated by Meta AI or sent by a person.
- **01:50–02:30:** A draft reply makes clear what will be shared and requires review; a moderation example distinguishes a sample decision from a real report or staff permission. The ending names “Future Muse Spark,” “Muse Voice Transcribe,” and “planned Meta integration — not connected in this preview.” It is candid but arrives too late to establish implementation. The closing line “A place to think, connect, and someday help someone else” is apt, though modest and unresolved.

## Authenticity, model attribution, and craft

The UI is unusually candid about simulation: it says replies come from local example rules, the community has no live activity or real membership, the moderator screen does not enforce staff permissions, and the prepared response is fictional. That avoids passing fake social activity off as real, while confirming that the journey is illustrative. The captions narrate a multi-week arc, but the interface gives the user room to remain uncertain. It moves rapidly from reflection to participation without showing how a real user would find and return to a community.

The closing slide attributes future structured understanding to **Muse Spark** and optional speech-to-text to **Muse Voice Transcribe**. It proposes a user-chosen topic and intent, correctable context, then permissioned retrieval with source and audience visible. That division is sensible, but no output or failure is attributable to either model. Correction controls, context cards, audience preview, and human moderation are visible but unverified. Captions provide context but often cover UI content and sometimes collide. I cannot assess sound. The calm typography is consistent; dense screens may be hard to read on a small display.

## Five highest-impact fix tickets

1. **00:00–00:15 — premise arrives late.** Replace the loading opener with a short, legible statement naming the user and tension, then show the private reflection screen. Verify a first-time viewer can explain the problem and product within 15 seconds.
2. **00:30–00:50 — captions obscure the strongest agency controls.** Reposition captions in a consistent lower safe area and hold the “Not confirmed yet” correction panel without overlap. Verify all controls and caption lines remain readable at mobile playback size.
3. **00:50–01:20 — matching looks asserted rather than demonstrated.** Show a user changing a context answer and the surfaced results changing with visible sources and a brief explanation. Verify that the same prompt yields a relevant, inspectable change and that “Not for me” alters the result.
4. **01:20–02:00 — community interaction is entirely illustrative.** Mark a clear transition from simulation to a real, consented test flow, or label the sequence as a design proposal and show a prototype boundary. Verify no viewer could mistake fictional accounts or prepared replies for live activity.
5. **02:30–02:50 — Meta integration remains a future claim.** Replace the closing architecture promise with an attributable Muse Spark/Voice Transcribe interaction, including user review, a correction, and an honest failure/uncertainty state. Verify the actual connected model and its limits in the submitted build.

**Skeptical judge objection:** This is a polished vision for a sensitive support space, not evidence that the proposed AI, retrieval, social, or moderation system exists. The care in labeling simulation is commendable, but the implementation score cannot be inferred from the quality of the mock workflow.

**Best-case reaction:** Harmonise has a humane angle: it helps a person separate their own question from pressure to produce an answer, then makes sharing a deliberate choice. The correctable interpretation, visible audience preview, and non-directive peer framing could make a useful alternative to advice-first support if a real system preserves those constraints.

**Release recommendation and blockers:** Do not shortlist this film as a demonstrated Meta-powered working submission. The explicit blockers are absence of connected model evidence, absence of a real end-to-end operation, and inability to establish any live community or moderation behavior from the film. Reconsider after a revised film shows one attributable model interaction and a real, consented workflow while keeping the current clear simulation boundaries.
