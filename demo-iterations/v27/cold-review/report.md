The final contribution stays in a fictional preview; no real transmission or integration is claimed.

## Meta model attribution

At 0:10–0:45, a prepared response offers a way to clarify the concern, and Arjun can reject and revise its reflection. But the film does not name distinct model roles, and says “no model connected”; it demonstrates neither a model call nor reasoning. Structured choices and an editable summary are prototype mechanisms, not evidence of inference. Community support is proposed, not observed.

## Accessibility and film craft

Large, high-contrast captions carry narration and caveats. Interface text is smaller, but central content is readable at 1920×1080 when paused. The restrained layout and steady sequence help comprehension; minimal motion also keeps energy flat. Audio could not be heard, so voice, music, mix, and caption fidelity cannot be assessed. The emotional high is Arjun’s correction and private choice; the explicit no-reply keeps the ending honest but quiet.

## Five highest-impact fix tickets

1. **0:10–0:45 — Distinguish proposed from running.** The prepared exchange can look live before the disclaimer registers. Keep “prepared local example · no model connected” beside it and briefly label the proposed AI role. This should prevent mistaken model-execution claims. Verify both labels are readable while the response is on screen.
2. **0:30–0:45 — Make correction legible.** The before/after is brief. Hold the first interpretation, “Not quite,” and revised reflection together, highlighting changed words. This lets viewers assess user control. Verify the full comparison is readable at normal playback.
3. **0:45–1:10 — Explain community value.** Filtering is visible, but why these experiences help is inferred. Add a short rationale linking the selected example to family context and naming a relevant difference. Verify it appears beside the example and fictional label.
4. **1:10–1:40 — Clarify sharing scope.** Alias, group, and exclusion details are dense. Enlarge a pre-share preview showing audience, alias, exact question, excluded reflection, and unchecked consent together. Verify all remain legible at normal playback.
5. **1:40–2:34 — Give the ending a concrete payoff.** “No reply yet” is candid, but the resolution is quiet. End on the private takeaway and choice to stop, tied to Arjun’s need, while retaining the no-reply/no-real-post disclosure. Verify the selected outcome and disclaimer are both visible.

## Skeptical judge objection

This is a carefully presented interaction concept, but prepared examples carry its apparent conversational intelligence and community value. The film says no model is connected, participants are fictional, and the thread has no reply. I can credit interaction design and safeguards, but cannot conclude the AI improves outcomes or that a real community will provide support.

## Best-case reaction

The film understands that sharing a sensitive family concern requires control and trust. Arjun can reject a framing, see a correction, consider an example while retaining the differences in his story, and edit the contribution before consent. Its strongest promise is a paced bridge from private thought to optional connection, including a clear choice to keep the result private. Candid labels make that proposal believable.

## Release recommendation and blockers

**Maybe; suitable for a concept/prototype showcase with its fictional and no-model-connected framing retained.** The film is coherent, and its privacy and agency details are persuasive. A stronger product-impact claim is blocked by the film’s evidence limits: no model execution, real participant, live community activity, or real reply/outcome is shown.
