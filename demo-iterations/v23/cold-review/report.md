## Executive decision: Maybe

Harmonise presents a considerate concept for adults under family pressure: privately reflect, correct an interpretation, then decide whether to browse peer experiences or share a bounded question. I would keep it in maybe. Strengths are visible correction, clear privacy framing, and candid labels for prepared examples and unavailable services. This screen walkthrough does not demonstrate running AI or real social activity.

## Authenticity, Meta attribution, and film craft

All visible dialogue is fictional or prepared. The linear story uses cuts between states, not a continuous live transformation. No live model, community activity, moderator, or bookable provider is evidenced. Captions narrate actions and limitations; with audio inaccessible, they are the only narration evidence considered. “Planned Muse Spark” supports a proposal, not execution. Structured reflection could help in a sensitive conversation, but no model-specific capability establishes necessity. Treat the local/prepared content as prototype behavior.

Text is large with sufficient contrast; motion is limited to transitions. “Not quite” is the emotional beat; the gentle takeaway leaves stakes unresolved. Captions are readable; sound accessibility cannot be judged.

## Five highest-impact fix tickets

1. **0:06–0:18 — Reply provenance.** A prepared reply appears without a visible cause. Show continuous input, submit, result, and a persistent prepared-rule/model label. This would distinguish example from generation. Verify with an uncut capture.
2. **0:24–0:39 — Correction proof.** Cuts obscure which action changes the reflection. Show “Not quite,” Arjun’s edit, and changed wording continuously. This would evidence agency and recovery. Verify the old/new values in one take.
3. **0:54–1:09 — Consent outcome.** The exact audience is previewed but consent remains unchecked. Show keep, edit, or share choices and a private cancel path; confirm only after consent. This would clarify control. Verify exact scope and resulting state.
4. **0:42–0:54 — Fictional social proof.** Ravi’s story may briefly read as live testimony. Keep a fictional label on story/reply screens and explain what live participation would add. Verify the label persists; evidence any live activity separately.
5. **1:09–1:21 — Support next step.** Unavailable roles are named without an action. Show a verified external-help route or a candid offer and safe exit. Verify the destination/status or exit/privacy state.

## Judge reactions

**Skeptical objection:** “This is a polished storyboard for a potentially useful reflection tool, but it repeatedly says examples are prepared and no model, listener, moderator, provider, or live activity is connected. I cannot credit core AI or social claims as operating product; fictional people also risk borrowing credibility.”

**Best-case reaction:** “The design recognizes that someone under family pressure may need time and control more than advice. Arjun can say ‘not quite,’ edit his meaning, browse without sharing, and preview exactly what a group would see. The film is candid about fictional content and unavailable support.”

## Release recommendation

Keep in **maybe**; do not shortlist on this film alone. The film supports judging the concept but does not substantiate live AI, active social participation, moderation, or verified support. These are blockers to operational credit, not claims that the prototype fails. A continuous correction capture, honest model provenance, and completed consent/exit path would improve the next film materially.
