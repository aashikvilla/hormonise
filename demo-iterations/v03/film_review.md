# Independent cold review — Hormonise v03

## Executive decision: Reject as a working submission; retain as a promising product concept

Hormonise addresses a specific need: someone under family pressure wants room to think, language for a difficult conversation, and experience without a verdict. The film charts private reflection into optional, audience-reviewed sharing, while marking its people and content fictional and stating that AI, messages, memberships, and moderation are not live.

That candor is also the blocker. Across 178 seconds, prepared exchanges and local example rules illustrate a product; the closing labels Meta integrations “planned” and “not connected.” No working model, community, moderation workflow, or backend is evidenced. This is a persuasive product vision, but not a demonstrated working submission. Reject for a build-and-deploy shortlist; reconsider after a bounded model-backed flow and human safeguards work end to end.

## First 15 seconds: comprehension and differentiation

The film opens inside the product at “You can start anywhere” (0:00), not with the user or problem. Arjun leaves a decision open; a caption contrasts “Advice: decide now” with “room to explain what’s happening at home.” Viewers must infer the family pressure and his need for a non-directive response. By 0:10, private chat, optional exploration, browser-only history, and 29 prepared exchanges are clear. Differentiation: private reflection can lead to scoped peer experience. A short opening card could name the user, pressure, and promise.

- **0:40–0:55 —** Finder contrasts broad legal matches with confirmed family context and explains the mismatch. Retrieval is described, not demonstrated live.
- **1:10–1:30 —** Four return days include corrected assumptions and an argument. “Being heard for part of it doesn’t erase the argument” avoids a false victory. Small text and long static holds flatten the walkthrough.
- **1:40–2:05 —** Exact audience, alias, question, excluded private context, and confirmation are visible before sharing. It ends at a prepared response; publishing and enforcement remain unshown.
- **1:55–2:15 —** Ravi’s fictional perspective says it was not generated by Meta AI or sent by a person. Arjun remains uncertain, a restrained emotional payoff.
- **2:35–2:58 —** Review screen says reports do not reach staff and permissions are not enforced. Closing diagram proposes Muse Spark and Muse Voice Transcribe, explicitly planned/not connected; no model request, output, or failure state appears.

## Authenticity, accessibility, and craft

Dialogue is prepared and linear, but on-screen labels clearly identify fictional history, participants, prepared exchanges, and no live activity. The argument persists and Arjun has no neat resolution. No live social or Meta capability is claimed; 2:40’s attribution is a proposal. Warm typography, green accents, and consistent panels create a coherent visual system. Burned captions are high-contrast and summarize transitions. Small UI text is difficult in wide shots; static holds aid inspection but flatten pace. Audio accessibility and delivery are unjudged. The most memorable beat is changing “a little time” to “a week.”

## Five highest-impact fix tickets

1. **0:00–0:10 —** Need and user arrive late. Open with a sentence naming family pressure and the promise of room to think. **Result:** immediate comprehension. **Verify:** ask a new viewer after 15 seconds who it serves and what it does.
2. **0:20–0:34 —** Interpretation is prepared/local. Show a connected model producing tentative structured output, user correction, and unavailable state. **Result:** prove model value and user control. **Verify:** compare online/offline runs and inspect editable output.
3. **0:40–0:55 —** Context rationale is narrated by UI, not proven by retrieval. Show context confirmation/rejection and live sourced results. **Result:** observable, correctable relevance. **Verify:** repeat under two confirmed contexts and compare sources/results.
4. **1:40–2:05 —** Consent preview stops before publishing. Show the exact audience/text published, private context excluded, and cancel leaving no post. **Result:** end-to-end privacy evidence. **Verify:** inspect test group state after confirm/cancel and attempt audience changes.
5. **2:35–2:58 —** Review is illustrative and permissions unenforced. Demonstrate role-gated report routing, audit outcome, unavailable state, and accurately labeled live model use. **Result:** credible safety/attribution. **Verify:** test reporter/member/reviewer access, routing, and model attribution.

## Judge reactions and release recommendation

**Skeptical objection:** “These are fictional screens; the safeguards are design intentions, while model claims are future tense. Why shortlist this over a smaller real interaction?”

**Best-case reaction:** “The team understands a sensitive need and counters premature advice, overconfident interpretation, accidental sharing, and false success. Correctable meaning, source-aware discovery, exact audience previews, and unresolved stories form a strong direction if real models and enforceable safeguards can preserve the restraint.”

**Release recommendation:** reject as a working submission; reconsider after an end-to-end model interaction and enforced privacy/moderation path. **Blockers:** no connected Meta model or live transcription; no live messaging, posts, or memberships; no real report routing or staff permissions; no backend, auth, or failure handling shown. These limits are disclosed, so the concern is execution evidence, not misleading attribution.

**Evidence mode:** reduced — MP4 picture and burned captions only. Audio playback was unavailable. No source footage, implementation materials, previous reviews, or sidecar text were consulted.
