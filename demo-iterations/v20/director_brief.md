# v20 creative brief: one room, one decision, one chosen boundary

## Direction

Earlier cuts treated the film as a sequence of explanations and interface pages. This cut follows one quiet, staged moment at a table: a performer portraying fictional Arjun pauses, tries a private reflection in the real frontend, corrects it, sees what a peer perspective can and cannot offer, and chooses a next step. Keep the camera’s attention on the person’s changing attention, with the interface as the object they are using. Use close, human-scale shots and motivated cuts between hands, face/shoulder, and the actual browser. No kinetic-text opener, product-story cards, synthetic app states, fake messages, or visualized model output. The screen recording must show the real frontend performing the visible actions.

The performer is not a testimonial or a real participant. They do not speak Arjun’s words; a calm third-person narrator carries the story. The film should make a person’s hesitation and relief perceptible without acting out a family confrontation or implying the prototype changed a real life. Show Arjun selecting **“Maybe later”** as the concrete next step, while retaining his private takeaway. Leave the family decision unresolved.

## Picture, narration, and disclosure rules

- Use only two prepared-fictional disclosure treatments: **(1)** “Fictional Arjun · performed scenario” from the opening, and **(2)** “Prepared local preview · Muse Spark not connected · no live peers or services” on the interface footage. Keep the second treatment adjacent to the reflection and peer example; do not add separate “fictional,” “illustrative,” or “prepared” badges for every card. The ending may state the factual preview status and that nothing was sent, without introducing a third fiction label.
- Capture actual frontend footage at readable scale and pair it with the performer in the same physical scene. Do not replace UI text, invent screen states, stage fake typing that implies live model execution, or fabricate replies, moderation, provider access, or a sent message. Every click shown must cause the displayed real state change.
- Make the story legible muted. Burn in all substantive narration as concise captions, synchronized to speech, with a caption-safe area that does not cover choices or disclosure text. Keep the narration observational, specific, and unhurried; do not narrate clicks or voice the fictional participant.
- Avoid static interface holds longer than five seconds unless the viewer is actively reading a privacy boundary. Let human reaction and purposeful frontend interaction carry the rhythm. No rapid cuts through dense text.
- Target **177 seconds** (acceptable final export: **176–179 seconds**). Review at 1920×1080 and 960×540. Verify that audience/problem/promise are clear by 00:14; the reject-and-edit action and changed words are visible; the peer example’s relevance and prepared status are clear; the Ravi preview appears once and no consent/send occurs; **“Keep privately”** and **“Maybe later”** both reach selected states; and the ending does not imply resolution, persistence, or live support.

## Acceptance gates

1. A muted first-time viewer can identify the intended audience, dilemma, and private/correctable/optional-peer promise by 14 seconds.
2. A viewer can point to the exact action that changes the reflection, name the changed idea, and say Arjun chose it. The prepared text cannot be mistaken for a Muse Spark result.
3. The film’s visual center includes a human performer throughout the decision, while captions make clear that Arjun is fictional and the portrayal is staged. No testimonial, real-user outcome, or family resolution is implied.
4. A viewer can explain what the peer example contributes, what differs, whether it is live, what the Ravi preview includes/excludes, and whether anything is sent.
5. The proposed Muse Spark role is stated once as future work to organize possible themes into a user-reviewed draft. The film never claims connection, execution, model quality, or an AI-generated answer.
6. The final selected state is **“Maybe later”** alongside the private takeaway; both are legible at normal playback and the film ends with the situation unresolved.
7. Export is 176–179 seconds, captions are synchronized and unobstructive, no unexplained still exceeds five seconds, and the finished video contains no fabricated live-service evidence.
