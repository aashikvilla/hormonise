Use v13 as the stronger story source. Its fictional family-pressure dilemma is specific; the “Not quite” correction and changed reflection make agency visible; the prepared status is candid; and the ending protects a private choice. Its persuasion score was **80** (clarity 20, journey 22, role value 21, craft 17). What held it back was the long sequence of dense screens, not the emotional premise or the agency mechanism.

The v20 score fell to **70** (17, 19, 19, 15). The performer did not supply evidence of a real interaction or outcome. The carefully planned Ravi comparison and selected “Maybe later” were still mostly claims carried by narration and cuts.

The v21 score rose to **72** (22, 18, 15, 17): the opening became clear, but role value collapsed. The v21 release check confirms 177.5 seconds, full decode, synchronized stream metadata, the consent/no-send state, and readable top disclosure/captions at 960×540; it also finds fine body copy small at half size. The cold judge and release verifier could not audition AAC audio, so narration intelligibility and audio-led craft remain unverified. The release check also confirms the opening promise is visible by 6 seconds, so v22 should spend its opening proving the user action rather than adding more explanation.

**Keep from v13:** the exact family-pressure conflict; the actual user correction as the story’s hinge; agency and privacy; prepared-example candor; the unresolved family decision. **Drop from v20/v21:** actor or generated-human framing as a proxy for evidence, split-screen montage, explanatory page-by-page coverage, repeated privacy/provider cards, and the suggestion that a proposed model or peer network has already helped. v22 is an evidence film: the viewer watches one uninterrupted frontend action cause one visible change, then sees what a prepared peer example can and cannot add, and one bounded choice inside the preview.

## Dramatic question and claim boundary

**Question:** Can a person change the wording of a private reflection until it belongs to them, then choose what to do with it?

The only demonstrated result is a state change in the local frontend during this preview. It is not a real-life outcome. The scenario and all peer material are prepared and fictional. Muse Spark is future work: it could organize themes into a draft for a person to review, edit, or discard. It is not connected, and the prepared reflection on screen is not model output. No real people, real accounts, model calls, live peers, service activity, credentials, sent messages, or external outcomes appear or are implied.

## Picture grammar

- The film is built around **one continuous, uncut 55–65 second capture of a real local frontend session**. Keep the cursor, typed words, correction action, and resulting changed reflection visible in the same uninterrupted take. Start before typing; do not begin on a prefilled result. Use the actual frontend’s supported flow and its real state changes. If the interface cannot show typed input changing the reflection in one take, capture only the behavior it really supports and state the limit plainly; do not stage the missing transition.
- Film the interaction full-screen. No actor, generated portrait, split screen, stock human, fake chat, incoming activity, invented animation, or decorative UI. The user is represented by their visible hands/cursor and the words they type. Keep the camera still; let the action make the scene move.
- After the main take, use only two evidence inserts: one prepared fictional peer example with its match and difference visible together, and the final selected preview state. Use a simple hard cut and a persistent timecode/progress line to show sequence. The evidence label is adjacent to each claim it qualifies.
- At 960×540, the active field, changed words, disclosure, peer comparison, and selected result must each be readable without pausing. Crop to one relevant panel at a time. Use UI text at least 24 px at 1920×1080 (12 px at 960×540); keep burned captions at least 44 px at 1920×1080 (22 px at 960×540). Never put captions over controls, edited text, or disclosures.
- Silent viewing must carry the complete argument. Burn in concise captions for every substantive spoken line; one caption at a time, no more than two lines, with short phrases and enough hold to read. Keep disclosure language short and repeated only when the evidence changes.
- Provide a clean narration/music mix in the export and a separate review copy or listening check for a reviewer with audio access. The prior cold review could not audition AAC audio, so v22 must not depend on vocal warmth, music, or sound effects for persuasion. Record that limitation in release notes; score the silent picture and captions on their own.

## Evidence and release checks

1. **Continuous action:** retain an unbroken source capture from first typed character through the changed reflection. A cold viewer must be able to identify the entered words, the correction, and the resulting difference without narration. If any transition is a cut or prefilled result, remove the “continuous” claim and disclose the actual sequence.
2. **Peer relevance:** the match and difference appear side by side in the same readable frame, with the prepared fictional provenance adjacent. The narration must not call this a real testimonial, a representative finding, social proof, or an outcome.
3. **Model attribution:** “future / proposed” and “not connected” appear in the same beat as the role statement. Do not label any current text as AI-generated or Muse Spark output.
4. **Choice and result:** show one action the user takes inside the preview—keeping the takeaway privately—and its selected state. Identify the result as preview-session-only. Do not add a second selected choice. Do not claim durable persistence or an external consequence.
5. **960×540 proof:** inspect the first frame, typed sentence, correction, changed result, peer comparison, audience/consent state, selected takeaway, and closing choice at 960×540. No essential text may require pausing, zooming, or reading a tiny full-page UI.
6. **Captions and audio:** every spoken claim is captioned in the safe band; verify timing, contrast, line length, and control clearance on muted playback. Separately audition the final AAC track for intelligibility, clipping, and sync where a reviewer can actually hear it. If the review channel cannot expose audio, state that limitation and do not claim it was heard.
7. **Duration:** final picture 176–179 seconds; captions finish by 176 seconds; no unexplained still or disclosure-only hold exceeds five seconds.
