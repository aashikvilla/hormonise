## Five highest-impact fix tickets

1. **0:00–0:15 — Identify Arjun and stakes sooner.** Add a line naming Arjun and the family pressure before navigation; verify viewers can name person, problem, and promise by 0:10.
2. **0:25–0:51 — Keep AI role and status together.** Add “Planned: suggest themes; Arjun decides. Prepared example; no model connected.” Verify viewers distinguish proposal from execution.
3. **0:30–0:51 — Hold the correction comparison.** Use a steady two-state close-up highlighting the correction and changed reflection. Verify viewers can identify both without narration.
4. **1:19–1:30 — Enlarge the sharing preview.** Hold a crop of alias, group, exact text, exclusions, unchecked consent, and cancel. Verify viewers can recall audience, content, exclusions, and unsent status from one view.
5. **0:51–1:19 — Simplify the transition into peer examples.** Replace multiple pages with one labeled prepared-peer-perspective card and the caveat about what does not carry over. Verify viewers understand its limits.

## Judge reactions and release recommendation

**Skeptical judge objection:** This is thoughtful interaction design shown with prepared content. The film does not demonstrate that Muse Spark interpreted anything, a real community exists, or a post can be sent. The correction state is useful prototype evidence, but it cannot establish real model quality or peer moderation. Dense small text also asks viewers to do considerable reading without an audible track I can assess.

**Best-case reaction:** The product lets a vulnerable user set the pace. Arjun can say “not quite,” replace the system’s framing with his own words, and see the reflection change. The peer example marks where its circumstances differ, and the sharing preview exposes audience and content together while preserving an unchecked consent state. “I can ask for time before deciding” is a useful payoff without pretending to resolve the family decision.

**Release recommendation: Maybe / conditional shortlist.** Credible as a frontend prototype film, with strong visible correction and consent controls and explicit prepared-content labels. No live model or community claims are substantiated; that blocks deployment or social-proof claims, not frontend-phase technical credit. Improve the opening and hold the correction and consent details at a larger scale before release.
