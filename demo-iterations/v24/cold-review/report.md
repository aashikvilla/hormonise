## Accessibility, skeptical objection, and best-case reaction

Typography is calm and large in the opening and prompts, but community screens pack text into one viewport; slower scrolling or magnification would help (0:42–1:23). Cursor motion is modest. Overlays are readable but cannot establish narration content. I cannot assess spoken pacing or sound mix. The emotional high point is Arjun’s corrected statement at 0:30; the film closes on a quiet choice rather than a resolved exchange.

**Skeptical judge objection:** This could be a carefully designed static prototype around prepared text. The film does not establish the planned AI role, a live peer network, or whether branches work beyond the recorded example. The benefits shown may come from editorial examples and privacy copy rather than the model or network.

**Best-case reaction:** The team treats interpretation and disclosure as choices the user should control. The interface makes uncertainty, correction, audience, and refusal visible, and does not present peer experience as a verdict. If these paths carry into a real service, the design could help people explore difficult decisions without being pushed toward a conclusion.

## Five highest-impact fix tickets

1. **0:00–0:05 — Opening density.** Replace the multi-card overview with one sentence naming the adult facing family pressure and “Reflect privately; share only if you choose.” Verify at 0:05 that problem, audience, and promise are clear.
2. **0:10–0:35 — Prepared interaction could be mistaken for AI.** Keep “prepared example · no model connected” visible during correction and label it a prototype demonstration. Verify viewers distinguish prototype behavior from model output at 0:30.
3. **0:35–0:42 — Matching rationale moves quickly.** Hold on the confirmed-context card and connect the corrected preference to the result. Verify that the film attributes this example to context, not scores.
4. **0:50–1:00 — Story evidence is text-heavy.** Highlight a short excerpt and one “what may not carry over” comparison. Verify the illustrative source and limitation remain legible without pausing.
5. **1:07–1:23 — Show the whole consent branch.** Preview exact audience, then select “Maybe later” and show the private state. Verify what would be shared and what stays private, without implying a real message was sent.

## Release recommendation

Keep the entry at **Maybe**. The film shows no blocker in its privacy framing or prototype flow; its main evidentiary blocker is that it cannot support claims of live model use, real participants, or an operating community. The next film should retain the candid labels while tightening the opening and making correction and consent branches easier to follow.
