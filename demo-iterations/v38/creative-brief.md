# Harmonise v38 — creative brief

## Creative aim

Make v38 feel materially different from v37: keep its real, continuous correction as the film’s evidence spine, then build a short story around the question **“What would help Arjun choose his next step?”** The answer is not a model, group, or directory. It is the words Arjun corrects, the perspective he can consider, and his choice to keep the reflection private for now.

Target **99 seconds** (acceptable 90–105; hard cap 120). Open with the specific family-pressure hook and a concrete product promise. Name **Muse Spark** in the first 15 seconds and explain its proposed, narrow role in on-screen copy: **“Planned Muse Spark role: organize possible themes for Arjun to review—not decide what is true.”** Keep **“Local prototype · no model connected”** adjacent and readable. This is disclosure of a future design, not evidence of a call or model output. The correction shown is a real, recorded frontend interaction using prepared local text; say so plainly.

The v13 persuasion score was 80, v36 was 79, and v37 was 70. These are single-judge results with differing evidence and moderate to low confidence; audio was inaccessible in every report. They show that the correction, specific hook, bounded choice, and clear no-send boundary have persuaded before, while static screen tours, tiny UI, and late/absent model attribution have repeatedly hurt. They do not establish a causal explanation or guarantee an 85. Use v38 to test the specific changes below, then compare another independent cold review and its evidence mode.

## Framing, readability, and continuity

- Master at **1920×1080**. Use the actual frontend as the picture, with tight, clean crops around the relevant interaction. At 1920, keep the active UI text at least 32 px where possible; disclosure and status labels at least 26 px; captions at least 34 px. Keep captions in a dedicated dark lower band and out of the controls, corrected words, consent state, and cursor path.
- The 960 review is a downscaled 1920 master (**960×540**). Design for it directly: essential UI text should remain at least 16 px after downscale; disclosure labels and captions at least 18 px. Do not shrink the full browser into a large empty canvas. Crop out browser chrome and unrelated panels. If a single screen cannot show audience and consent legibly, use two adjacent crops of the same actual state; do not fabricate a transition or imply another action.
- Keep the model label in a stable, high-contrast title strip for the complete model-role beat. Do not place it over the correction text or captions. Keep the prepared/local and no-model labels visible throughout the actual correction interaction, not only on the opening card.
- The continuous correction remains a source recording of the current frontend. Preserve its genuine cursor and timing; do not overlay a fake loading state, model response, or typed words. The optional share branch may show active cursor focus as evidence of the preview choices, but consent must remain unchecked and Continue disabled.
- Use visual motion only to follow a real user action: cursor selection, edited words, changed reflection, audience/draft focus, or **Maybe later**. Avoid decorative camera movement across static pages. Make each transition advance Arjun’s decision.

## Truth and claim boundaries

The scenario and people are fictional; community cards and discussion copy are prepared examples; the frontend is local; no model is connected; no peer, moderator, provider, booking flow, or messaging service is live; nothing is sent. Keep each disclosure alongside the relevant material. Do not suggest that the proposed Muse Spark role has run, that Arjun’s corrected reflection was produced by a model, that peers replied, or that any outcome occurred. The strongest demonstrated claim is: **a fictional person can correct prepared local text in the frontend, inspect a bounded sharing preview, and choose to wait with the reflection private.** Preserve the community’s possible value as human perspective without presenting it as an available live service or a guaranteed response.

## Measurable review gates

Use five cold viewers who have not seen earlier cuts; show the first 15 seconds once, then ask what Harmonise does, for whom, what Muse Spark would do, and whether a model is connected. Pass if at least four can state the private/correctable reflection promise and adult family-pressure audience, and all five understand Muse Spark is planned and unconnected. Ask what changed during the correction; all five should identify Arjun’s own words and none should say the model generated the shown answer.

At normal playback on 1920×1080 and 960×540, verify that the corrected text, prepared/no-model label, peer-fiction label, exact audience, private-context exclusion, unchecked consent, disabled Continue, no-send status, and **Maybe later** choice can be read without pausing. At least four of five viewers should recall that the optional share was not sent and that Arjun chose to keep time to decide privately. Check the final 10 seconds: viewers should describe Arjun’s choice, not a recap of screens. Review the export muted and with sound; confirm captions match every spoken cue, no caption covers critical UI, duration is 90–105 seconds, and the same final choice remains visible through the last frame.

A later blind score can indicate whether this version worked for that reviewer and evidence setup. Treat 85 as a target for learning, never a promised result; report category scores, confidence, timestamped evidence, and whether audio was accessible alongside the aggregate.
