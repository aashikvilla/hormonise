This film presents a thoughtful concept for adults facing family pressure. Its strongest choice is the boundary between private reflection and later sharing: Arjun can correct a reading, inspect the audience and alias, and keep his draft private. ## Authenticity, model attribution, and film craft

The film labels itself fictional/prepared and says “no model connected” and “no live community.” At about 0:25–0:30, it says Muse Spark could suggest a theme while Arjun decides whether it fits. The correction and peer comparison are prepared examples; the film substantiates a proposed interaction pattern and prototype states, not model calls or social activity. The journey says the relationship outcome remains unresolved, and the final choice keeps the thought private. Captions are frequent, large, and high contrast; small interface text is harder to read. I cannot assess narration or sound design because I could not hear the AAC audio.

## Five highest-impact fix tickets

1. **0:10–0:30:** Separate prepared response from proposed AI. Add a readable label: “Prepared response; planned Muse Spark role: suggest a theme for the user to accept, edit, or reject.” Verify it remains visible beside the response and controls.
2. **0:25–0:40:** The correction is not clearly shown as an observed action. Show one continuous prototype capture of Arjun editing, confirming, and seeing changed context. Verify before/after text without a cut.
3. **0:40–1:00:** The community sequence may imply real participation. Keep “fictional prepared example; no live activity” and label Ravi’s account provenance. Verify the qualification is legible on the story itself.
4. **0:50–1:00:** Hold on the consent preview so audience, alias, question, exclusions, unchecked box, and cancel action can be read at normal playback. Verify all fields in one full-frame shot.
5. **0:00–1:20:** Replace some static holds with close views of the hook, correction, and privacy choice, returning to full UI for context. Verify captions stay unobscured and decisions readable at normal speed.

## Judge reactions and release recommendation

**Skeptical objection:** Prepared dialogue and fictional community stories do not show that a model reflects accurately, users trust it, or peer support works. The film admits those limits and should not be mistaken for a live AI demo.

**Best-case reaction:** The product gives people room to clarify wishes before advice or disclosure. Correction, privacy, and a self-chosen next step are central; peer material warns that another person’s circumstances may differ. That restraint could help someone approach a hard conversation.

**Release recommendation: Maybe / shortlist discussion.** The film is clear and ethically cautious enough to merit a closer look at the prototype. On this film-only record, the blockers are absent connected-model evidence, live community activity, and any outcome beyond a private draft. These limits describe what this film substantiates; they do not impose a live-backend requirement on this frontend-only phase. Audio could not be assessed.
