# Independent cold review — Harmonise v04

## Executive decision: reject for shortlist

Harmonise presents a thoughtful product vision for people facing difficult family conversations. The film shows a concrete path from private reflection to carefully chosen community support, with meaningful user control at the sharing boundary. I would reject it as a working-submission shortlist candidate on the evidence in this film, for three reasons:

1. The experience is explicitly a prepared, fictional browser preview. At 0:03 and again across the film, the interface says replies come from local example rules and “no AI service is connected”; the ending calls both Meta integrations planned.
2. The central product claim—AI that helps people clarify what they mean without taking over—is demonstrated through fixed sample dialogue, not a working model interaction or a model-attributed result.
3. Community, posting, reporting, moderation, and staff review are all simulated. The film itself says the sample creates no account, sends no report to staff, and has no live moderator.

This is a persuasive product concept, with a separate film-persuasion score of 79/100. The evidence score is 68/100. Confidence is medium-high (0.84): the UI and disclosure are consistently legible, while I could not play the audio in this review environment, so voice performance and mix are not judged.

## First 15 seconds: comprehension

The first screen says “You can start anywhere,” promising private reflection or exploration at one’s own pace. By 0:03, fictional Arjun feels ungrateful asking family for room to think. The contrast—“Advice: decide now” versus “What Arjun needs: room to explain”—differentiates the product from advice-giving chatbots. The need is specific, but the film gives no evidence that this service improves outcomes over existing support.

## Scene audit

At 0:03, “fictional sample history” offers Day 1, 4, 9, and 16 visits; history stays in the browser tab and restart clears it. This suggests a revisitable experience, but the 29 prepared exchanges make the opening feel staged.

At 0:28, “Have I got this right?” separates what was said from what might matter and offers “Not quite” and “Edit in my words.” Transcript review says transcription is disconnected and no audio will be shared. This is the strongest idea: interpretation stays tentative and editable. The cut comes before any revised response proves the correction loop.

At 0:43, community discovery contrasts a broad legal-paperwork match with confirmed family-expectations context and offers “Not for me.” At 1:18, the group says “Reading available / No live activity”; joining creates no real group or membership. This avoids fake social proof but confirms the social outcome is unimplemented.

At 1:51, “Ask Ravi in the group” previews the group, alias, and question, excludes private conversation, and leaves a “nothing is sent” box unchecked. At 2:08, a reply draft previews audience and text but remains unpublished. These are strong consent patterns; real identity, deletion, abuse, and recovery remain unexplained.

At 2:40, community review shows a report and revision request but says no reviewer is reached and staff permissions are not enforced. At 2:53 the end card calls both integrations planned. This is credible but ends on roadmap disclosure, with no human payoff.

## Authenticity and Meta attribution

Dialogue is fictional and prepared; the film’s sequence is linear despite selectable days. Fake activity is not passed off as live: group, host, posts, and staff actions are marked illustrative or historical. Subtitles are readable on gray backing but cover page text, while full-page shots make details small.

At 2:53, Muse Spark is planned for “dialogue and structured, correctable context”; Muse Voice Transcribe for optional speech-to-text reviewed before sending. These roles fit the need to express uncertain thoughts safely. Correction buttons and previews substantiate the intended mechanism, not model use. No call, attribution, latency, refusal, error path, or model-versus-rules comparison appears. The interface says local rules answer and no AI is connected: both integrations remain proposed.

## Accessibility and craft

The restrained interface is polished, with clear headings and consent boundaries. Captions remain legible but cover lower controls; detailed full-page shots need closer crops. Static capture suits the sensitive subject, though long holds slow the middle. I could not play the AAC track, so I score this area conservatively and make no claim about voice, music, or mix.

## Five highest-impact fix tickets

1. **0:24–0:35 — prove correction.** The tentative reading and transcript appear, then the film cuts away. Show real input, model interpretation, “Not quite,” and revised output. This would prove the core mechanism; verify a meaningful correction and attributed response in a fresh session.
2. **0:03–0:20 — establish first use.** The 29-exchange history makes the case feel preloaded. Start with a fictional but grounded trigger and a user choosing what to share. Verify cold viewers understand the need and first action within 15 seconds.
3. **1:12–2:35 — show functional states.** Joining, asking Ravi, drafting, and moderation are sample-only. Demonstrate persisted opt-in, preview, cancel, and report states, clearly labeling remaining mocks; verify no action appears to reach a person when it does not.
4. **2:38–2:53 — end on agency.** Replace the simulated report and roadmap-only ending with a reversible choice, such as saving a draft, plus a clear status card. Verify viewers can name the choice and what is planned.
5. **Across 0:00–2:58 — improve legibility.** Captions cover controls and full-page shots shrink text. Use closer crops and a consistent caption band. Verify ordinary laptop playback is readable without pausing.

## Judge reactions and release recommendation

**Skeptical judge:** “This is a compelling deck disguised as a product film. The hard parts—sensitive-data handling, a safe model, real peer participation, operational moderation, and failure recovery—are not present. The video is candid about that, but candid simulation still does not establish technical depth or a working Meta integration.”

**Best-case judge:** “The team understands that a vulnerable person needs time and agency, not a confident answer. The editable interpretation, explicit audience preview, and permission to read without replying show a more careful social product instinct than a generic advice bot. If those safeguards survive a real model and real community, the concept could matter.”

**Recommendation:** do not shortlist this as a completed implementation on film evidence. The explicit blockers are absent model integration, unverified persistence and privacy behavior, no live community or moderation path, and no demonstrated failure handling. Re-review after a working end-to-end interaction shows model output attribution, correction, consent, and a recoverable failure state; keep the sample-only status visible until then.
